* Lean result and exact abstraction boundary

[[file:../../../dev/proofs/evidence/protected-signer/ProtectedSignerRelease.log][Twelve printed theorem dependency reports]] contain no =sorryAx=, new axioms or
final compilation errors. Results use no axioms or standard =propext=,
=Quot.sound= and inherited =Classical.choice=. Checked statements include:

- Every released statement retains a matching immutable protected commit row
  and a prefix covered by the nonrollback promise history.
- Equal-position statements released by the same signer cannot conflict.
- Lost responses and service restart cannot erase committed rows; a new service
  epoch does not make an old key-service handle current.
- Delivered statements must have been released already; late delivery creates
  no new statement. Root publication requires matching delivered quorum evidence.
- The initialized product refines the earlier promise/configuration/execution
  product, preserving original burn, dispatch and account-application uniqueness,
  shared capacity and unknown original reservation retention.

The product models an atomic promise-plus-row decision as an event with exact
prior/next protected histories. Native CAS, crash durability and cryptographic
response authenticity are owner contracts, not derived machine-code facts.
SignerOwner receipt/recovery predicates are distinct from root Store predicates;
a valid root image alone cannot qualify a signing service. Full source-image
encoding/signature binding, immutable membership authentication, key custody and
physical queue/session revocation remain unproved native obligations.

Quint bounds three voters, six IDs and three prefix positions, with term/revision
inside each candidate cell. Lean uses arbitrary finite structural record
histories and Nat-indexed requests/voters over the initialized prior model.
There is no generated equivalence proof between Quint, Lean and a native signer.
Repeated signatures for the identical prefix are deliberately allowed; no
exactly-once cryptographic computation is claimed. Sticky unresolved promises
may block forever. Existing native account/transport assumptions are inherited,
not discharged by the additional list-prefix proof.
