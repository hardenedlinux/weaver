#+TITLE: Independent Commit Floor and Recovery
#+LANGUAGE: en

* Decision and evidence boundary

=Project.sysml= and =WeaverExecution= remain authoritative for responsibilities.
=PolicyEvidenceDesign.CommitCut= and =FloorDecision= specify target data, not a
second execution engine. Extend the existing =PublicationRecovery= proof owner;
reuse existing account, provider, continuation and history validators. No
dependency pin or production Gateway implementation changes in this review.

The independent floor protects the original assignment and four complete
prefixes: publication, consumption, account applications and history. A
publication-only floor cannot detect rollback of an already spent claim. Prefix
lengths alone cannot detect replacement of an application body at the same
ordinal. Entries bind ordered canonical identity, body, scope, predecessor and
domain-owner validation evidence. A production digest representation requires
domain-separated canonical encoding and an authenticated collision-resistant
commitment; the models use full values, not a trusted string label.

Independence means a Gateway snapshot cannot restore the authority's decisions,
epoch or transport burns. Separate files, processes, Kubernetes identities or
storage labels do not prove this property. If the authority itself rolls back,
the safety theorem does not apply. Authority quorum, durable-prefix and
reconfiguration obligations remain with their existing owners. This is a
logical execution-right boundary for an AI Agent Gateway; no physical-isolation
or HSM deployment requirement is introduced.

* Commit and recovery protocol

| Stage | Required deciding operation | Crash or lost-response interpretation |
|-------+-----------------------------+---------------------------------------|
| Prepare | Durably retain the original command ID/body, original scope, owner epoch and complete expected predecessor cut; candidate WAL is inert | A prepared row cannot publish Policy, release funds, advance Session or send |
| Decide | Independent authority CAS checks current cut, epoch, source custody, exact command identity and existing domain validation; append the decision and committed record atomically | Both committed and definitive rejected outcomes are immutable; a timeout is neither |
| Inspect | Authenticate the original outcome and complete canonical command; repeated submission resolves the same original result under the deciding transaction | A committed historical outcome is not the current cut; an absent result remains unknown |
| Resolve old intent | Serialize a late commit against a durable epoch/fence rejection; preserve original ID and obligation until the result is definitive | Reading absence and allocating a new nonce can race a late commit and is forbidden |
| Materialize | Fetch recoverable committed bodies and repair all four projections, including account application ID plus delta, claim/reservation consumption and history closure | Incomplete, conflicting or unavailable bodies retain the original holds and deny activation |
| Activate | Re-read authority and match the full repaired cut, owner epoch, original assignment and resolved intent set | Recovered local active flags and cached receipts grant no authority |
| Admit effect | Sole Rust transport owner persists a one-use burn, then rechecks the exact current cut/epoch under its final admission lock through the bounded send callback | A cut/fence change between burn and callback omits the send and retains the unknown hold; it does not refund or mint a replacement permit |

Final authority CAS must resolve an existing ID before making another decision,
inside the same serialization boundary. Exact duplicate workers recover one
outcome. A stale parent receives a durable rejection; inspection must not rebase
it. Reauthorization is a new decision linked to the original claim, profile,
budget and history domains after definitive reconciliation. A new operation ID,
nonce or Authority identity cannot reset a logical claim or account reservation.

All relevant mutations invalidate activation. Current reads are observations,
not bearer permits. The actual transport owner must participate in authority
serialization in the independent recovery domain; otherwise a worker can read a
current floor, restore an old image or wait through revocation, and send later.
The production design must establish a linearization boundary spanning actual
socket admission and floor/epoch changes. An offline token or an ordinary remote
read followed by a local socket call does not establish that boundary.

The persisted burn and actual network call are separate events. A crash between
them may produce no call; recovery cannot infer no-execution or reissue the
ambiguous burn. The live transport-owner continuation binds its original cut,
epoch, logical key and one-use admission; it is not reconstructible from the
presence of a persisted burn. Queued provider-client retries and provider redirects remain
disabled. Stream range release reuses the existing per-release current-state
guards. Bytes already admitted may drain. A partial write or missing usage
receipt cannot prove final usage, no-execution, or a refundable reservation.

Floor decision retention and committed body availability are separate duties.
Retaining a root without reconstructible records safely stops progress; it does
not authorize activation. Garbage collection/checkpoint truncation must preserve
the existing manifest and prefix-availability proofs. Lack of a response,
timeout or temporary absence is never a deletion or budget-release certificate.

No domain-validity Boolean crosses a trusted API as a client assertion. Quint's
=qualified= and Lean's =DomainValid.assess= denote authenticated validation by the
existing domain owner at final CAS. They are explicit refinement premises, not
proof of Cedar evaluation, account arithmetic or semantic history completeness.

* Reused proofs and counterexamples

| Existing owner | Checked extension / reused law | Premise or uncovered refinement |
|----------------+--------------------------------+---------------------------------|
| [[file:../../../dev/proofs/quint/PublicationRecovery.qnt][PublicationRecovery Quint]] | Four full prefixes, immutable outcomes, absent/late commits, fenced rejection, exact repair, changed epoch, same-length body replacement, duplicate logical consumption and account application | Finite exploration; full canonical key/scope mapping must be implemented |
| Same owner | Separate durable burn and actual admission; restart discards live continuation; a fence between them leaves hold unchanged | Atomic admission action represents the sole transport owner; no socket-level consensus is proved |
| Same owner, unsafe actions outside =step= | Local-only activation and rollback of authority burns violate safety | Authority rollback is an explicit excluded premise, never silently classified as ordinary restart |
| [[file:../../../dev/proofs/lean/PublicationRecovery.lean][PublicationRecovery Lean, CommitFloor namespace]] | Any finite trace preserves all four ordered prefixes, original outcomes, scope, spent keys and unknown holds; stale cut/epoch/unresolved intent prevents activation | Full values abstract canonical bodies; authority state is non-rollback; no new account evaluator |
| [[file:../../../dev/proofs/lean/LlmWalActivation.lean][LlmWalActivation]] | Reuse =actual_account_composition=, =original_reservation_once=, =account_conservation=, =committed_prefix_retained= and =unknown_budget_hold_retained= | The concrete floor-account-journal projection and authenticated source-ready binding remain required; prefix preservation alone does not prove balances |
| Existing =AuthorityPublication= / =ProviderRpc= | Keep current read-to-final-CAS and per-release guards; historical receipts cannot reopen a spent claim | Whole-store rollback remains unsafe until the independent protocol is integrated |

Lean's floor trace is parametric in a domain-validation predicate and, for owner
replacement, an authentic exact-incarnation stop predicate. Its burn law
proves preservation of spent status, not that a remote provider executed exactly
once. The existing account theorem requires the real source reconciliation
binding before releasing an unknown hold. These premises are not discharged by
record equality or by retaining a checksum. No claim of a complete machine-to-
network refinement proof follows from the composition envelope.

* Executable acceptance and current results

The modular [[file:../../../crates/ledger/tests/unit/commit_floor.rs][SQLite protocol reference]] uses actual transactions, durable outcomes,
separate restorable projections and the real Ledger dispatch API. It is compiled
only as a test submodule. The fixture deliberately limits restore to the Gateway
store. It does not establish deployment independence, credential custody,
authenticated source assignment or a production floor service.

| Scenario | Executed receipt | Safety result / limit |
|----------+------------------+-----------------------|
| Committed response lost before projection | Close/reopen authority; inspect each original command; replay historical result | Exact outcomes survive; no rebase to later cut |
| Partial and substituted projection | Delete history row; replace account body while keeping row count; repair full rows | Activation rejects both incomplete and wrong-body images |
| Absent, late and old-epoch outcomes | Query absent prepared intent; allow delayed commit; rotate before another delayed commit | Unknown blocks activation; old epoch gets immutable rejection |
| Concurrent identical workers | Two independent connections submit the same frozen ID after a barrier | One prefix append; both recover exactly the same result |
| Stale parent / fresh nonce / fresh application ID | Submit competing and freshly named commands for the same original key | Neither claim consumption nor application repeats in the reference protocol |
| Gateway snapshot and service restart | Restore pre-consumption Gateway DB twice, reopen floor, call actual Ledger dispatch only through floor owner | One callback total, original hold four retained; no actual provider billing asserted |
| Fence after burn before callback | Inject epoch change between the two transactions; retry with new epoch | Zero callbacks, burn remains spent and hold four retained |
| Existing production negative control | Keep =whole_database_rollback_requires_an_independent_recovery_floor= | Three callbacks remain possible without this protocol; this is expected unsafe evidence |

* Comparative metric and next implementation boundary

The comparison metric separates specified coverage, executed component evidence
and production acceptance. This round adds a testable four-prefix recovery
protocol and six reference tests; integrated adapter acceptance remains 0/13.
No performance gain, compression ratio or provider cost reduction was measured.
Previous [[file:59.29-strands-box-cross-review.org][Box]] and [[file:59.25-openshell-and-tao3k-ecosystem.org][OpenShell]] source comparisons remain the baseline;
their current implementations were not re-audited in this protocol-only round.

| System | Benefit to retain | Weaver gap / resolution |
|--------+-------------------+--------------------------|
| Weaver | Higher-order Scheme Governance plus schema-bound Cedar evidence can constrain current execution and recovery | Prove and implement the independent authority-to-transport boundary; preserve unknown holds at every unresolved seam |
| Box | Reuse the reviewed request-validation and result-recording lessons | Those mechanisms alone do not discharge Weaver's original assignment, shared budget or rollback contracts |
| OpenShell | Reuse the reviewed isolation and operational observation lessons | Operational observations do not replace authoritative commit outcomes; export telemetry under explicit Policy |

Next production work requires agreement on the actual independent authority and
transport-owner topology, authenticating source custody, durable record/body
availability and the concrete account/history refinement. Keep Go's cloud-native
control role and Scheme's semantic role; Rust remains the sole final executor.

The follow-up [[file:59.43-floor-transport-owner.org][floor-to-transport review]] selects the base target topology and
defines its actual admission linearization and no-overlap handoff. It also
strengthens the test reference to compare the proposal's explicit observed cut;
repairing a shared projection must not silently rebase a queued old proposal.
