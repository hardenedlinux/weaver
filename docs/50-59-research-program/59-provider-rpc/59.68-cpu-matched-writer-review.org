* Native results and retained regression

The later [[file:59.69-hot-path-cost-boundaries.org][cost-boundary review]] traces new reproductions without retroactively assigning
their exact phase percentages to this historical 0.74 sample.

MRR Data [[https://github.com/tao3k/mrr-data/pull/12][PR 12]] commit =b305f22= owns the native target updates and
[[https://github.com/tao3k/mrr-data/blob/b305f22/docs/benchmarks/shared-backend-cpu-matched-reuse.org][complete sample table, component timings and reproduction commands]]. Weaver consumes that
record; it does not maintain another benchmark implementation or storage backend.

The current Host reports 12 available CPUs. Both paired campaigns use 12 runtime,
write/shared workers and reusable capacity, with 16 blocking threads. The native
lifecycle target now uses that allocation and 12 concurrent shutdown callers.
Other standard test runtimes retain their existing four-worker minimum.

Each campaign completes 18 cases and 13,824 commits with exact recovery, no
refusal, Unknown or unexpected failure. Combined: 36 cases and 27,648 original
operation recoveries. A single fixed additional campaign checks stability after
the first showed latency variation; neither replaces the other. The compiled
benchmark binary is unchanged between campaigns. Task-owned builds/tests do not
overlap timed measurement; the Host remains shared and OS caches uncontrolled.

Campaign B round 1 retains a hot-domain throughput regression: reuse/WAL=0.74;
P95=11.45 versus 5.96 ms; P99=65.97 versus 9.48 ms. Independent-home round 1
in both campaigns also has a worse reuse P95 despite higher TPS. The record
must not summarize these results as a universal WAL replacement or tail win.

The regressing hot pair's mean physical envelope is 95.96 microseconds for reuse
versus 102.18 for WAL. Its get cost is 155.81 versus 120.55 microseconds; other
transaction-body cost is 145.75 versus 85.39. Body and scheduling/validation delay
remain relevant. These observations do not identify a Host-jitter, fsync or
pager cause. The separate native phase diagnostic supports the existing fresh
connection first-BEGIN/disposal cost, but does not explain the reused hot tail.

Keep reuse explicit and WAL available for ordered hot domains. Next causal
acceptance requires a dedicated-Host trace separating queue/validator/native/I/O
delay under the same original identities and fixed workload. Do not weaken FULL,
split original obligations, retry implicitly or switch storage modes automatically.
No production provider change occurred in this round; differences from historical
measurements cannot be attributed to another optimization.

* Recovery evidence and open Host obligations

Five CPU-matched lifecycle tests cover six schedules using native transactions:
cancel before validation, cancel before COMMIT after validation, cancel after a
known commit with lost delivery, exact historical replay while delivery remains
pending, effective retirement before seal and competing pending retirement.
Shutdown drains accepted work and closes once. Reconstruction retains original
operation/authority/outbox bindings and held=4; retired authority cannot use a
new nonce. Driver Unknown disposal and late lease returns retain their existing
unit controls. A free slot never implies a budget refund.

The reusable standard Arrow/query + graph/publication suite passes 78 tests,
including existing independent-process crash/partial-tuple recovery and ASP deny
checks. Strict all-target Clippy passes. CPU-matched provider reconstruction
and the separate existing process-death controls are different evidence classes.
The WAL-control and fresh-MVCC standard configurations also pass 63 tests each
with this CPU-matched lifecycle target. Opt-in measurements are counted separately.
Timeouts and full cleanup remain unchanged; remote CI is not inferred from local
success. No production Weaver code, dependency pin or qualification package changes.

SPEC semantics and existing Lean/Quint roles remain authoritative. Physical
power loss, whole-store rollback, independent retained roots, external authority
effectiveness and actual Rust transport currentAtUse remain Host/executor proof
obligations. These fixtures preserve an immutable unknown-exposure body; they
exercise neither an account-refund algorithm nor external sending.
