#+title: Weaver Pending Policy Changes, Authority Publication and Effective Acknowledgement
#+language: en
#+options: toc:3

* Core decision and meaning of effectiveness

An external producer cannot make a Policy/history change effective independently
and notify the final consumer asynchronously. The recommended protocol stages
a source-bound intent, commits the change at the final consumption authority,
and only then acknowledges that exact committed change to the producer.

The authority commit is the effective ordering point relative to consumption.
Producer staging is pending data. Producer acknowledgement is evidence that the
change committed at a particular generation; it is not a claim that this older
change is still the current tip. Confirmation never resets the current Policy,
reissues a permit, changes the original semantic contract or releases a hold.

This is logical execution-authority separation for an AI Agent Gateway. No
physical-isolation device is required. A qualified non-rollback authoritative
log and authenticated source/owner receipts remain deployment contracts.

* Persistent objects and immutable identity

| Object | Required identity and contents | Owner |
|--------+--------------------------------+-------|
| Staged change | Stable change ID, admitted authority domain/source vector, exact original binding, expected parent generation/tip, content commitment and semantic decision | Durable producer intent store with approval/source evidence |
| Authority publication | Exact staged change, committed generation and current publisher fence | Rust consumption authority and its durable conditional log |
| Producer acknowledgement | Exact attributable authority publication and its historical generation | Durable producer acknowledgement store |
| Readable projections | Available staged rows, owner prefix and acknowledgement rows | Recoverable caches; never the source of authority |
| Consumption image | Original binding, ledger revision, current fence and current published Policy generation | Existing final CAS authority |

Change ID binds the full operation and payload, not only the desired Allow/Deny
value. It must not be rebound after a lost reply. A new source namespace, nonce
or worker cannot redirect an existing change to another original claim, profile,
budget or history domain. Typed source identity and domain membership must be
authenticated; numerical equality alone is insufficient.

The Lean model binds each staged change to the actual original registry intent,
the admitted source vector and the original source history domain. It models one
original task and one external authority domain, with arbitrary finite publication
lists. Quint uses a bounded source fixture and a small sampled change-ID set.
A shared multi-task authority domain still needs its earlier identity/lineage
contracts; this delta does not claim to verify that complete product.

* Three ordered durable decisions

| Phase | Preconditions | Result |
|-------+---------------+--------|
| Stage pending | Authenticated original subject/domain, approved content and durable fresh stable ID | Immutable producer intent; no effective-state or execution grant |
| Publish at owner | Exact staged object readable, change not already published, expected parent generation/tip equals actual committed tip, current publisher fence, actual publication Policy, owner online, qualified durable commit | Append exact publication and advance effective Policy generation/value atomically |
| Acknowledge to producer | Exact publication is in the qualified owner prefix, exact staged object is available, producer acknowledgement durably recorded | Historical committed-generation evidence only |

Staging approval is not a cached publication grant. Publication rechecks the
authenticated actual change, current source state and worker through
=owner.publishValid=; denial or indeterminate authorization blocks the append.
A stale stage approval cannot substitute for that check.

Only owner publication projects to category 15's =policyChange=. Other source
transitions must leave Policy generation/value unchanged. Thus producer callbacks,
cache restoration and ordinary worker progress cannot bypass the publication log.
The effective value and generation always equal the committed log tip and length
in the initialized abstract model. Real implementations must bind that position to
namespace, authority lineage and authenticated prefix identity, not just a counter.

Publication shares the final consumption serialization domain. A consumption
before publication follows the earlier effective contract; consumption afterward
must match the new generation and all current original constraints. Old permits
fail even if an Allow/Deny/Allow sequence restores the same Boolean. Permission
restoration cannot override original history denial, scope limits or spent-state.

The exact parent check prevents two pending changes from silently overwriting
one another. The winner commits once; the other remains stale and cannot be
automatically rebased. Reconciliation must expose the conflict. A new approved
change may name a newly observed parent, but cannot erase the original pending
identity, unknown consumption or original obligations. No timeout cancellation or
pending garbage-collection protocol is introduced here.

* Lost replies, retries and late confirmations

| Failure window | Required recovery | Prohibited shortcut |
|----------------+-------------------+---------------------|
| Stage commit loses reply | Query/restore the exact stable producer intent | Reuse the ID for another payload or infer absence from cache |
| Owner commit loses reply | Query the exact change and authority prefix; return the existing publication if present | Apply again, allocate a new generation or retry based on empty local visibility |
| Ack commit loses reply | Restore/query the producer record or repeat the same attributable acknowledgement | Treat acknowledgement loss as owner rollback |
| Older publication confirmation arrives after a newer change | Preserve it as historical evidence with its original generation/content | Set effective state to the older Allow or revive its old permit |
| Concurrent publisher with stale parent | Report conflict against the actual current prefix | Automatically reinterpret the change under the new predecessor |
| Old publisher resumes after owner change | Reject its old fence; current owner may reconcile the same original intent | Trust its existing connection or durable-receipt possession as a fresh writer grant |

The modeled stage/publish commands are fresh-only mutations. Retries first use
exact authoritative lookup; an existing decision returns data instead of a second
append. The producer may repeat acknowledgement delivery, which is semantically
idempotent: it does not change the owner log or effective state. Native storage
may deduplicate acknowledgement rows by the exact publication identity.

A record saying =Committed(change, generation)= must not be presented as a current
permission lease. If a caller needs current status, it must read the current
qualified owner prefix and compare the full publication identity. Every actual
consumption still uses the final CAS; no confirmation API produces a send grant.

* Cross-store snapshots and owner recovery

Producer intent storage, authority log and producer acknowledgements can lose
local visibility independently. Their projections may be restored at different
ages. The following recovery rules keep these mismatches safe:

1. Keep stable authoritative contents separate from readable projections. Loading
   a producer or acknowledgement snapshot does not assign the owner generation,
   effective value, claim, profile or ledger state.
2. Restore a staged intent only from its exact durable source identity. Reconstruct
   missing producer state from qualified evidence rather than inventing an intent.
3. Reconcile the owner publication by exact change ID and payload, committed parent
   and qualified current prefix. An empty local view is not proof of no commit.
4. On owner restart, advance the logical fence, close owner operations and require
   qualified recovery of the exact authoritative base state and publication log.
   Existing source-ledger recovery gates also remain mandatory before consumption.
5. Restore acknowledgement visibility from exact committed records. An old record
   can remain valid history while the current effective Policy is different.

The formal =recoverOwner= step does not load an arbitrary snapshot into authority.
It reopens access only under =owner.recoveryValid= over the exact current source
state and durable publication log. In Lean, the prior source restart also retires
old child admission and marks execution unrecovered; owner-log recovery alone
cannot manufacture the source's execution-recovery proof. Quint abstracts that
source recovery behind its qualified recovery fixture.

The durable log and its anti-rollback witness must survive the cache/worker
snapshot failure domain. If the entire trusted publication log, ledger and witness
roll back together, the deployment violates this premise. Native recovery must
verify complete committed prefixes and exact namespaces, including unresolved
operations; it cannot merely accept matching local counter values.

* Pending restrictions and response semantics

Before owner publication, a submitted restriction is explicitly pending, not
already effective. The submission API must expose that status. A producer that
requires immediate revocation must synchronously reach the owner publication
point or place the relevant owner path into a qualified closed state. A separate
producer database write followed by asynchronous notification does not meet this
protocol. Keeping a change pending is an availability limitation, not permission
to falsely acknowledge effectiveness.

The abstraction does not model a distributed outbox implementation, lease expiry,
cryptographic commitments, consensus, fairness or cancellation. The native adapter
must preserve the status distinction and stable query identity across restarts.
The existing POO Flow attempt representation has bounded generation/revision
checks; native code must reject exhaustion or perform an explicitly qualified
lineage transition. Wrapping a generation to reuse old authority is not supported
by the unbounded-natural proof.

* Original obligations and final sending

Publication changes the effective external authorization context. It cannot
replace the original task binding or semantic profile. The composition retains
original claim uniqueness, original budget/history domains and allowance bounds.
An old Allow acknowledgement cannot mint a new claim, reset a consumed nonce or
turn unknown usage into zero. A late qualified usage receipt still reconciles the
original child slot; original settlement and account-application protocols apply.

Final consumption validates the complete current image and actual source effect.
Already accepted transport bytes are not retracted by later publication. New
messages, ranges and retries still cross their existing gates; no producer ack
creates another tail capability. This design does not claim remote exactly-once.

* Responsibilities and performance boundary

| Owner | Responsibility |
|-------+----------------|
| Scheme / POO Flow | Original subject and domain semantics; approve source-bound staged change and define status/obligation meaning |
| Go | Cloud-native change submission and reconciliation; preserve stable IDs, pending/conflict status and historical receipt attribution |
| Rust authority owner | Fenced parent-CAS publication, atomic effective generation/log update, exact lookup and final consumption serialization |
| Rust broker | Enforce current full image and actual effect; keep spent-state, range and unknown-hold constraints |
| Cedar | Authorize the authenticated stage/publication/use operation under every required layer; reject errors or indeterminate evaluation |
| Quint / Lean | Check protocol failures and compose actual existing execution transitions under explicit durable-owner premises |

Keep immutable profile/certification caches on the fast path. A producer
acknowledgement is outside the final consumption critical path. The owner commit
must include effective generation publication before acknowledging effectiveness;
asynchronous producer delivery is then safe. Measure pending age, parent-CAS
conflicts, publication/lookup latency, effective-change lag and recovery backlog.
No throughput or native durability result is claimed here.
