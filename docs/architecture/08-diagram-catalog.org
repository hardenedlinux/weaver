* Purpose and evidence boundary

Architecture, interaction flows, state transitions, and trust boundaries use
D2 as their canonical diagram language. English Org remains the documentation
format; =.d2= sources and generated =.svg= previews are diagram assets. Diagrams
make guards and ownership reviewable. They do not implement or prove the
protocol, establish process isolation, or qualify the physical data path.

The architecture views describe Weaver's design. Existing mechanisms and
required extensions are distinguished by the source register and X1-X10 map
in [[file:07-source-admission-ledger-fencing.org][Source admission, durable ledger, and fencing]].
The root, Core, and Lambda Aitia revisions in
[[file:01-reference-comparison.org][the evidence register]] remain separate source identities.

W17 makes the logical key independent of issuer and stream incarnation. Its
observation/accounting chain has no return edge to claim or offer. Quint and
Lean form a design/proof rail outside the request path. Exact model actions,
theorems, results and premises live in [[file:../../dev/specs/README.org][the formal artifact guide]], not graph labels.

W01 containers group logical responsibilities. A Go adapter inside the runtime
role group does not mean Go executes inside a Rust process. The Zero Trust
panel is a normative legend rather than an additional service. The dashed Quint/Lean links in W01 are semantic and state-machine design
contracts, not evaluator RPCs or proof checking on the request path. Go discovery and
reconciliation carry scoped observations; they do not mint Policy authority,
choose a ledger fence, or reopen a dispatch claim. Scheme owns composition and
source/lifecycle semantics. Rust is the reference physical coordinator and
broker placement, subject to the qualification requirements in the prose.

Sequence diagrams order the selected interactions. Flowchart layout and edge
position do not imply transaction atomicity or timing. W02's reserve, durable ConsumePending and deliver are distinct and ordered;
its consume and W10's ledger commit are separate operations. Its coordinator actor
represents a service with separate bounded tasks, not reentry into one blocked
coroutine. The selected synchronous callback profile requires qualified finite
leases and an admitted own-transition derivation without reentering Scheme. W03 distinguishes positive consumption knowledge from durable send permission
when a later commit cannot succeed. W04 deliberately shows one race outcome; the
normative decision table and W09 also cover a competing claim and lost abort
replies. W07 is an evidence/record view, not a replacement admission state
machine: NoSubmissionProven never unspends a claim, a business result does not
close the emitter, and closure does not prove zero usage. W08 separates the
existing Cedar decision semantics from the required error-free execution gate;
the current runtime issue path does not yet enforce that stricter gate.
W05 distinguishes rejection of stale database mutations from stopping an
already accepted network operation. W06 requires a new evaluated admission
family; the existing unknown preflight cannot authorize a runtime effect.

W11 separates shared semantics, bounded model exploration, Lean proof
obligations and physical guards. Its runtime column names the Scheme source owner,
Go integrations and Rust coordinator/ledger/broker rather than a generic verifier. Its joins are required correspondence and
admission obligations, not an existing automatic proof pipeline. FM1-FM12
remain pending; documentation rendering never satisfies G6.

W12 makes consume, S4, S5, transport and independent observation boundaries
reviewable across concrete events, model actions and proof/evidence obligations.
Rows are correspondence obligations rather than atomic operations. The claim requires
a concrete exclusive authority; stronger effect-count assertions require a
complete attributable execution trace.
The caption and owning review distinguish limited guard-law checks from missing
Weaver integration proofs.

W13 focuses on permission through physical send. Its authority evaluates Cedar
and issues the scoped grant; Cedar is not itself a grant issuer. The broker
receives the unique local emission handle, binds actual bytes/peer/credentials,
and refuses submission on a failed final guard while retaining the spent claim.
The wider Go and assurance relationships are in W01 and W11.

W14 shows the exact adapter/broker boundary. SDK construction has no upstream
send authority; the broker verifies immutable input, consumes/commits/claims,
and gates actual request starts and admitted stream ranges. Its input range
arrow does not bypass the selected per-unit semantic checks or reservations.
Redirect/retry responses are proposals, never a new handle. This is a logical
view: consume, durable commit and claim remain separate S3/S4/S5 transitions.

W15 specializes the stream path: complete message authority precedes durable
queue eligibility, physical enqueue and typed claim. Parent escrow splits and
queue credits are distinct from local reserve/consume. The writer's bounded
OfferIntent and the stream seal are ordered by the ledger; the socket remains
a separate physical boundary. A pre-cut ticket can outlive seal observation.
Its retirement path ends in held/settled/unknown accounting and preserves spent
claims; no edge returns an old message to queue eligibility. The reference has one authorized message
slot/writer per stream and allows concurrent inert staging/independent streams.

W16 distinguishes policy semantic root, execution root and authoritative state
revision. A pure candidate is inert until the exact snapshot/command CAS commits;
unknown results preserve spent state. The committed fact and original issuer
positive consumption join at the same canonical operation before obligations,
typed claim and final transport gates. The view describes contracts, not a new
mandatory shadow-token layer: P12/P13, P15 and P5 keep their actual distinct
protocol identities. Restart recovers facts/obligations without restoring handles.

* Diagram content boundary

Architecture views show responsibilities, interfaces, trust boundaries and
state transitions. Version pins, commit IDs, build/test counts, audit findings,
source-register IDs and qualification status belong in Org evidence tables.
Keep those out of component labels and diagram footers. Code as Policy views
must explain why Cedar is in the permission path and what still controls the
actual effect. A test receipt is not a component of the target architecture.

* Enterprise presentation conventions

The visual references are D2/Terrastruct's [[https://terrastruct.com/examples/1/][architecture example library]] and
[[https://d2lang.com/examples/tala/][official TALA gallery]]. The shared render Library supplies the engine and visual layers; this is not a claim
to use a vendor-certified Enterprise template or a paid feature.

Choose a visual grammar for the subject. Responsibility maps use ownership
zones; routing shows candidates and one selected provider; state views emphasize
irreversible transitions; sequence views use native actors and messages; data
views show records and joins; assurance views separate independent obligations.
Readable type, deliberate spacing, semantic status accents and local technology
marks provide continuity without forcing every view into identical panels.
Keep refusal/unknown outcomes recognizable by labels as well as colors.
Coordinates have no protocol meaning.

Use brand marks only to identify the actual technology in architecture headers.
The Go wordmark replaces the rejected mascot treatment. Go, Rust and Kubernetes
assets are bundled locally with [[file:diagrams/assets/icon-sources.org][sources and byte digests]]. Native D2 geometry,
including ledger cylinders, identifies generic components. Do not mix flat
illustration packs, mascots and oversized status icons into the same view.
Presentation ownership lives under [[file:../../dev/d2/README.org][dev/d2]], with distinct imported
profiles in =dev/d2/themes=. The shared base only supplies typography and semantic
status conventions; each view chooses its own profile and layout grammar.

W02 and W10 split one long sequence at its positive consumption observation.
They are the same original attempt, source lease and synchronous callback.
W08 folds invalid/stale/missing/error/deny branches into one labeled evaluation
boundary exit; the predicate and CX cases retain the individual failure causes.
A full-resolution [[file:diagrams/01-system-boundaries.svg][PNG overview]] accompanies the SVG for convenient preview.

* Authoring and reproducible rendering

Keep one D2 artifact per view. README D2 is generated from the SDK's SysML
interpretation and Lean references through Go; edit the source contracts rather
than its generated semantic copy. Other corpus views retain their declared
source ownership until migrated. Org embeds the generated SVG and links the D2;
do not maintain an independent Mermaid copy of the same view.
Change semantic prose, source labels, state guards, and acceptance references
in the same revision. Generated previews must be refreshed after source changes.

The default TypeScript entrypoint consumes the local shared render Library.
D2 retains visual choices; the README projection's questions, responsibilities
and relationships come from SysML and the source-bound proof mapping. Public library APIs own declaration
reading, standard D2 rendering, composed styles and HTML/PNG export. Weaver adds
question-specific responsibility, relationship-matrix and proof compositions,
captured local assets and graph-binding verification. Generic layout, font
outlining and card-renderer implementations have been removed from Weaver.

Run from the Weaver repository root:

#+begin_src text
go -C go run ./cmd/weaver-dev render-diagrams
#+end_src

The Go owner uses the current host CPU count, validates source-selected profiles,
publishes library-driven PNGs and full model SVG references, and retains
source/output identities in =.cache/d2=. A changed source image or failed render
cannot produce a passing receipt. No Python, Bash or extra runtime service is used.

When updating the local library, record the captured package integrity and review
regenerated views. Export checks reject declared region clipping and figure
overflow; graph checks reject lost source identities and rebound endpoints.
These checks do not establish that every label pair is separated. Visual
inspection remains necessary for hierarchy, reading order and readable size.

* Review and validation obligations

- Every added state must identify its durable owner, transition guard, and
  recovery disposition in the owning Org contract.
- Every authorization arrow must identify exact operation binding, currentness,
  lifetime, authority generation, and source guard requirements in the prose.
- A timeout never implies non-consumption or safe refund. A new worker never
  receives a replacement emission handle for an already claimed attempt.
- Lease expiry and database fencing never imply that a paused old process or
  an accepted socket operation has stopped. Label physical enforcement separately.
- English labels, resolvable local links, formatted D2, successful validation,
  regenerated SVG, and visual inspection are required documentation checks.
- Map security cases to the existing AC, R, G, Z, and SF registers in
  [[file:03-performance-and-validation.org][Performance and validation]] and
  [[file:04-zero-trust-and-review.org][Zero Trust baseline and review]]. In particular,
  SF1-SF12 cover bridge, timeout, restart, rollback, and stale-worker obligations.
  CX1-CX18 cover this cross-review's callback, diagnostic, race and record cases.
  A rendered diagram does not mark any of those acceptance cases as passed.

The SF and CX cases remain pending implementation and execution. No runtime, native
acceptance, model checking, proof rerun, throughput, or latency result follows
from these documentation checks.
