#+title: Subject Authority, Producer Assignment and Enrollment Plan Publication
#+language: en
#+startup: overview

* Finding and chosen authority boundary
An independent subject must have a configured authoritative source. A shared
Kubernetes service account, a caller-selected OIDC issuer, a forwarded bearer token
or a public key with a self-signature cannot assign an enterprise agent subject.
The Gateway accepts identity facts from a tenant-controlled authority configured
outside the workload's request path. Its issuer/tenant/purpose trust policy and
stable subject mapping are administrative inputs, not request parameters.

The authority can be an existing enterprise identity directory or agent identity
service. It must authenticate its own enrollment administrators and enforce who may
create, renew, rotate or revoke a subject credential. This design does not assert
that such a service is implemented in POO Flow or cedar-poo-spec. Initial identity
provisioning, trust-anchor administration and native authentication remain explicit
trust-root premises. Repeating a Gateway enrollment request is not a provisioning
ceremony and cannot allocate a new independent subject.

Stable subject identity is the canonical tuple of authority namespace, tenant and
an immutable issuer object identifier. Display names, email addresses, Kubernetes
service-account names, pod names and reusable account names are not canonical IDs.
Native encoding must be injective and distinguish deleted/recreated issuer objects.
The formal Nat index abstracts that canonical tuple; Principal also carries authority
and tenant fields checked against the original task. Source import is authenticated,
audience/purpose constrained and bound to the configured namespace. TLS termination
metadata is trustworthy only when supplied by the authentic trusted peer boundary.

* Separate identities and authorities
| Identity | Authoritative owner | Permitted change |
|----------+---------------------+------------------|
| Stable subject | Configured identity authority | Never renamed by token refresh, session reconnection or worker restart |
| Subject authentication credential | Identity authority under an independently authorized renewal/rotation policy | Current generation advances; a verification key may remain the same or change |
| Producer principal and credential | Configured producer authority | Credential generation/key may rotate while retaining producer identity and delegated scope |
| Original task and allowed producer | Trusted POO task producer/coordinator | Immutable canonical task-to-subject and task-to-producer assignment; no client-selected alias or new budget/history domain |
| Enrollment public key | Original task commitment, then immutable assignment | Cannot be replaced by subject-token or producer-key rotation |
| First beneficiary key and bootstrap session | Existing beneficiary-registration protocol | Remain frozen after first binding; credential recovery grants no rebind |

The producer is separately authenticated and authorized for the exact original task.
An authentic producer credential for another tenant, another principal or a broader
unrelated task is insufficient. The subject must also authenticate its current
credential, and enrollment-key possession must cover the same exact assignment
transcript. A producer cannot use a shared workload credential to nominate a new
subject or enrollment key. The original task source fixes both the intended subject
and allowed producer before this protocol accepts an assignment.

A public source record containing a new generation is not a usable credential.
Native verify must authenticate an actually valid credential for that generation,
plus subject/producer/enrollment proofs over the complete transcript. Merely copying
a new fence or proving possession of an unchanged public-key pair is insufficient
when the credential has been retired. Credentials shared with the same private keys
remain the same logical principal; the Gateway cannot distinguish physical processes
holding identical authorized material. No physical isolation or process-uniqueness
claim is introduced.

* Original task and immutable producer assignment
The authoritative Task contains the original Plan template with subjectKey erased,
canonical launch, stable subject, tenant/authority, allowed producer, original Root,
enrollment key, issuance session/challenge, audience and enrollment-only purpose.
Erasing the subject authentication key permits selection of the current subject key
before the assignment; it does not erase the enrollment key or original task semantics.

Assignment binds that Task to one exact Plan, including the selected subject
authentication key. Its final CAS checks the exact original Task, empty canonical
assignment slot, current subject and producer source records, full joint proof,
current policy and observed outer revision. The native log retains the command,
proof references and authentic source snapshots as well as the determining outcome.
Model maps are projections; inductive transition witnesses retain the guarded event.
No credential, invitation, custody capability, LLM request or budget refund is created.

The assignment slot is immutable. Lost response, new token, new credential ID,
new nonce, new worker, MCP/A2A/Client ACP reconnect or new session cannot reopen it.
Recovery locates the same original canonical launch and returns the exact recorded
assignment or unresolved status. A restored absence is not a new first-writer right.

* Credential rotation and safe reauthorization
Historical provenance and current approval are separate. Subject and producer source
records carry immutable identity coordinates and a strictly increasing credential
generation. Rotation compares the exact current parent, preserves the stable identity,
requires authenticated authority evidence and advances the generation. Old-parent
writes, generation reset and cross-tenant/issuer replacement are rejected.

| Change | Required treatment | Forbidden shortcut |
|--------+--------------------+--------------------|
| Subject credential renewed with the same authentication key | Retain the original assignment; obtain fresh current subject/producer/enrollment proof and append approval for that exact assignment | Reuse old approval after only refreshing a read or copying the new generation |
| Producer credential/key rotated | Authenticate the current producer in the original delegated scope; reapprove the identical assignment | Treat a different producer principal as the original producer |
| Subject authentication key changed after assignment | Preserve the assignment and enrollment key; frozen Plan fails closed | Rewrite subjectKey or enrollment key in the pending assignment |
| Subject authentication key changed before assignment | A new assignment may select the authentic current subject key while preserving the exact Task template | Derive a different canonical subject or enrollment key from the key change |
| Identity credential revoked | Reject new assignment/publication/issuance that lacks current authentic identity evidence | Consider an authentic historical signature to be current approval |

This category provides same-key subject renewal and producer-key rotation continuity.
Automatic migration of a frozen Plan's subject authentication key is deliberately
not provided. Such migration requires a separately authorized protocol preserving
the original task, enrollment key, first beneficiary binding, unknown reservation
and history obligations. Availability after key loss is not proved here.

Source rotation invalidates the outer observed revision and old approval snapshots.
Fresh approval may replace an old approval for the same immutable assignment, but
cannot replace that assignment or erase its pending intent. Historical published
Plans remain evidence after rotation. Pure historical joins do not require their
old credentials to remain currently valid.

* Plan publication and subsequent issuance
Plan publication joins the exact assignment and fresh approval with current subject
and producer rows, native proof, current Cedar/source approval, predecessor epoch,
original Blueprint and expected outer revision. The actual predecessor Plan append
and determining publication outcome are one logical authoritative event. The entire
predecessor successor is fixed to a Plan/revision update; enrollment credentials,
ID index, signing requests, enrollment roots, beneficiary bindings and execution
state cannot change as hidden publication side effects.

Checking only Plan publication leaves a gap: identity credentials can rotate between
publication and credential issuance. Every newly created enrollment credential must
therefore pass an additional current assignment/source/approval gate at the final
outer CAS, as well as the actual predecessor issuance guards. It must also authenticate
this issuance caller with a current subject credential, bound to the exact credential
body/ID, current source generations, outer revision and fresh channel/challenge. A
stored current approval is not a bearer credential for a caller that lacks this proof. NewIssues exposes this
obligation in Lean; the Quint issuance wrapper checks it explicitly. A refreshed raw
predecessor read cannot substitute for a complete joined outer image or live approval.

An already issued enrollment credential is a separate authority object. A routine
identity-token rotation does not erase its historical authority or automatically
revoke every existing enrollment capability. If Policy requires revoking those
capabilities, the coordinator must execute the existing actual enrollment/issuer
revocation transition and downstream fences. It must not claim that source-record
rotation alone recalls already released credentials or transmitted bytes. Historical
account reconciliation remains allowed through the original evidence chain; the
new-issuance identity gate does not impose a fresh login requirement on old unknown
budget settlement.

* Publication, recovery and cross-store loss of response
| Event | Durable authority result | Recovery and concurrency rule |
|-------+--------------------------+-------------------------------|
| Subject/producer import | Canonical source row with authenticated authority evidence | No caller-selected issuer; exact-parent rotation under the accepted prefix |
| Original Task publication | Canonical task-to-subject/producer and original enrollment commitment | Reuse the original task family, claim, profile, budget and history domain |
| Assignment commit | Exact original Task plus frozen Plan and outcome | Query the original launch after lost reply; concurrent loser cannot replace the winner |
| Approval commit | Authenticated current source snapshots for the same assignment | New source generation invalidates it; reauthorization appends rather than discards pending intent |
| Plan/outcome commit | Actual predecessor Plan and outer publication outcome | Partial projections and one-sided recovery are quarantined before reads authorize mutation |
| Enrollment issue | Exact original Plan plus current subject/producer approval | Recheck at this later CAS, not just at Plan publication |
| Recovery query | Current subject authentication and read policy | Return original outcome; add no mutation authority, currentness, key replacement or reservation release |

Source, task, assignment, approval and Plan/outcome records share one logical accepted
commit history. Physical tables may be projections in separate stores; independent
successful writes are not independent authority decisions. The final owner compares
all relevant source/assignment/approval revisions under its determining CAS. A signed
identity snapshot obtained before that CAS is not proof that it remains current.
Resolve unknown commits by original command and body against that same authoritative
history. A late response may report an old outcome, but cannot mark it current or
activate a new assignment.

The independent recovery floor must cover these outer source and assignment commits.
The account/source execution floor may remain unchanged during all of them. Restore
complete projection joins against the accepted outer prefix before issuing any absence
or mutation certificate. An old worker cannot refresh its authority by presenting the
current public generation or a locally restored source row. If the accepted prefix or
a determining row is unavailable, quarantine new enrollment work and retain the original
unknown hold. No pure image invariant detects coherent rollback of the entire history.

* Scheme, Go and Rust responsibilities
| Owner | Responsibility | Interface boundary |
|-------+----------------+--------------------|
| Scheme / POO Governance | Original Task semantics, canonical subject/producer scope, profile/claim/history/reservation binding, Cedar policy over authentic facts, explicit migration rules | Supplies exact original commitment and current policy/source approval; cannot manufacture identity from caller headers |
| Go / cloud-native adapters | Kubernetes eligibility, configured enterprise identity connector, protocol termination, credential validation/evidence collection and retry/status transport | Passes immutable typed commands and authentic channel/authority evidence; shared SA never nominates an independent subject or changes a canonical assignment |
| Rust / final authoritative owner | Source namespace/index ownership, exact-parent generation CAS, immutable Task/assignment, current approval join, Plan/outcome commit, later issuance gate and accepted-prefix recovery | Verifies actual native proof and live fact association at the deciding CAS; no adapter or callback can bypass it |
| Quint / Lean | Concrete races, rollback/projection faults and stale-worker controls / arbitrary finite preservation and actual predecessor composition | Neither replaces native identity provisioning, cryptographic verification, authoritative storage or transport qualification |

Native commands carry a canonical task/subject/producer reference, exact original
commitment, source credential generations and proof references, expected outer and
predecessor revisions, complete immutable body and outcome query identity. Proofs
cover domain separation, issuer/tenant/audience/purpose, task, subject, producer,
enrollment key, selected authentication key, both credential generations, challenge
and authenticated channel. They must not be reduced to independent valid JWTs over
unrelated statements. Native encoding, trust-chain administration, credential issuance
and rotation policy, counter exhaustion and accepted-prefix storage are acceptance
obligations. They do not require new dependency pins.

This is enrollment/control-plane work. Cache authenticated parsing/signature results
only with exact identity and credential-generation dependencies; do not cache a live
approval across rotation. Use a bounded per-task join certificate tied to the accepted
prefix and determining CAS rather than a registry-wide scan on the streaming path.
Existing final Rust broker checks still constrain routing, provider-client retries, partial writes,
unknown execution and budget settlement. No performance claim is established here.

* Verification boundary
The [[file:48.03-proof-and-source-evidence.org][evidence register]] distinguishes directly composed Weaver proofs from
inspected POO Flow/Cedar contracts. Native acceptance must test issuer/tenant confusion,
forwarded tokens and proof mixing, old credentials copying a new public generation,
concurrent source rotation and assignment/publication/issue CAS, lost commit responses,
mixed snapshots, coherent rollback below the accepted prefix, old-worker recovery,
first-key replacement and retained unknown budget. Safety is conditional on the explicit
trust roots and complete recovery prefix; native validation and recovery liveness remain
unqualified. No Gateway implementation or dependency pin changes are included.
