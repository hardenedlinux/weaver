* Lean result and composition boundary

[[file:../../../proofs/evidence/device-isolation/DeviceIsolationRecovery.log][Fourteen printed theorem reports]] contain no =sorryAx=, added axioms or final
compiler errors. Dependencies are none or standard =propext=, =Quot.sound= and
inherited =Classical.choice=. Checked results include:

- Newly accepted isolation joins the exact current retiring manifest, old floor,
  complete blocked coverage and authentic native certificate.
- Alias identities cannot retag; protected floor and committed history cannot
  roll back under any permitted transition.
- Every floor append preserves its exact original isolation cut; the initialized
  journal contains one committed row per protected floor advance.
- An accepted unresolved floor intent survives every reset/restart and may only
  resolve through its original committed cut; no implicit abort or rebinding.
- Validated callbacks retain the original current submission/producer envelope;
  every new export rechecks current hardware and original commit-chain context.
- The initialized product refines the prior key/signing/configuration/execution
  chain, preserving original burn/dispatch/account uniqueness, shared capacity
  and unknown original reservation retention.

The new layer maps reset/driver restart to the prior close transition, accepted
intent and historical-read events to stutters, and typed IO/control events to
qualified prior key transitions. Generic delegation cannot alter key floor,
isolation/readiness, queue/used-job state or exports behind the new boundary.

The floor row, protected successor and signer-session invalidation occur in one
abstract commit event. Authentic recovery includes full committed prefix and
outstanding accepted intent. Native write durability, alias-inventory completeness,
attestation freshness and cut/decision-versus-reset fencing are supplied owner
contracts. They are not derived from fsync metadata or a successful logout.
A sticky old intent may become unfinishable after reset and remain blocked;
no qualified abort/replacement or liveness theorem is supplied.

The native adapter must serialize the authoritative floor decision with its
qualified context fence. A decision before that fence may return after reset as
valid historical evidence; a decision ordered after the fence cannot newly use
the old cut. Unknown ordering is not activation authority. This is a required
native refinement, not a claim that a raw physical reset is atomic with storage.

Lean uses arbitrary finite structural cut lists and Nat-indexed aliases/jobs.
Quint imports the small original key model and adds nine alias IDs plus explicit
owned-entry/binding phases before device start. Its manifests are sets; Lean uses
no-duplicate-ID lists. Native canonical encoding, producer/key identity, callback
bytes and hash/prefix binding remain unproved implementation obligations. There
is no extracted equivalence or native DMA/IOMMU theorem.

Historical floor recovery and live physical qualification are separate. An old
committed cut remains valid history after reset; it cannot activate the current
device. The current-view arithmetic head is proof bookkeeping. Native receipts
must authenticate full content and publication identity, not equal counters.

The safe reset profile requires sealed-output confidentiality or a fail-closed
interlock through real reset/detection races. If an old reader can obtain
unreleased usable signature bytes first, that profile is false; the DMA trace
exposes it. No later software gate can retract those bytes. Complete rollback of
the protected owner/key domain is also outside the safe model. Unknown original
claim, nonce, budget/history and application obligations remain retained.
