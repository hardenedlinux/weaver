* Evidence interpretation and retained authoring failure

The key-bypass mutants preserve individually valid local records and stay
within the funded pool, while independent semantic claim and offer counters
reach two for one admitted intent. They fail the alias/semantic invariant.
The capacity mutants overbook a sum of unequal costs; the history mutant
actually offers after a common restriction. Successful negative tests mean
those expected violations were observed, not that the invalid design is safe.

wrongPayloadTest uses Quint's terminal fail combinator to assert that the
actual enroll action rejects an incorrect immutable payload. Its terminal ITF
entry has no s value because the rejected action supplies no successor state;
the preceding state is the last ledger state. This is simulator/test semantics,
not a record of a gateway losing or resetting persistent state.

An earlier authoring attempt inspected s after that fail combinator and produced
QNT502 (Variable s not set). An attempted explicit stutter then produced QNT202
(Multiple updates of variable s). The control was corrected to finish at fail,
as specified by Quint. The normal transition relation was not weakened, and
neither failed harness attempt is counted as a safety pass. The retained raw
failure below predates the final source identity table and is diagnostic only.
