* Interpretation of the controls

The settled-charge and three-message controls execute permitted authority and
accounting paths beyond the previous two-key scope. Migration/restart controls
retain original claims and tickets, admit late evidence and reject execution
by a new claim owner. Another root's shared-account allocation invalidates a
staged transfer even when the sealed root fence has not changed.

The stale-worker counterexamples can preserve local affine counts and budget
while violating current executor authority. The wrong-domain allocator uses
an empty second account to overbook the actual canonical account. The history
mutants select or clear a different history scope. The restored-checkpoint
variant uses a fresh nonce and performs a second semantic claim/offer for the
same logical message while its final local record again looks affine.

Audit ghosts represent specification history, not a hidden durable runtime
oracle. Restoring authoritative state together with its only freshness anchor
cannot be ruled out by local equality checks; the actual anti-rollback lineage
and physical fencing requirements remain explicit in the dossier.

The two rejection controls terminate in Quint's fail combinator. Their final
ITF entry has no successor s value; the preceding state is the last actual
ledger state. That is test-combinator semantics, not gateway state loss. Passing
negative tests mean that the expected invariant violations were observed;
the invalid transitions remain excluded from normal step.
